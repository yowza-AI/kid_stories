# CHAPTER MAP — *The Ledger of Ninefold*

**Arc slug:** `phillip-dragonhall`
**Hero:** Phillip Vane, 15, Quire, bond 33
**Sessions:** 7 × ~2000 words (12–14 min narrated), one continuous escalating arc
**Rating ceiling:** PG-13 — loss and dread, never injury. Every session ends soft, even when it lands hard.

---

## THE SPINE IN ONE PAGE

Phillip's stated quest is **retrieval**: buy back the four years his father sold. His real
engine is his flaw: **he pays.** The arc runs those two things at each other until they break.

| # | Title | Phillip's goal | What it costs him | The turn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Weight of a Day | Pass the Assize; save Nessa's place | The green door of Lock Four, with his father standing in it | Overpaying is praised. He is *marked.* |
| 2 | Down the Sluice | Find where spent coin goes | A birthday, and his first hidden arrears | Coin doesn't vanish. It silts. It can be held. |
| 3 | The Old Girls | Get to the bottom | Nothing paid — everything shaken | Dragons are people, filed. One of them knows him. |
| 4 | A Gift Freely Given | Speak with Ninefold | The gift (given, not spent) — and which bed is his | **MIDPOINT:** nothing bought comes back. His whole plan inverts. |
| 5 | The Spendhouse Ledger | Find who did this | The last of his slack; his idea of his father | His tuition *was* the purchase. Forty-one children. |
| 6 | In Arrears | Hold the seam; save everyone | Odd, Nessa, Hew, his place | **LOWEST:** his paying breaks other people. |
| 7 | The Reading | Stop the Grieving | No coin at all — only everything else | The dragon speaks in his own three-year-old voice. He chooses to give it back. |

**Escalation ladder (must be honoured in the prose):**

- **Cost of magic:** a doorway → a birthday + first blur → which bed is mine → a friend's own name → *no coin, and a far worse currency.*
- **The school's private face:** a Bursar's notebook → a bricked grate → the Sumps → the feed → the Spendhouse ledger → the binding offer → the arithmetic said out loud in daylight.
- **The dragons:** a sound below → warm grit → eleven eyes → a chorus → a name → a voice through the grates → ***I.***
- **Phillip's flaw damages:** himself (1–2) → himself badly (3–4) → his father's memory of him (5) → **Odd** (6) → and in 7 he refuses to pay and it costs more than paying ever did.

**The chain rule:** each session opens *exactly* where the last one ended. Nobody teleports; no
time is skipped without the previous end-state carrying into the first line.

## SESSION 1 — *The Weight of a Day*

**Slug:** `weight-of-a-day`

**Logline.** At the Michaelmas Assize Phillip pays four times the required price to keep Nessa's
place — and the whole Hall calls it magnificent.

**Goal.** Pass the Assize, and keep Nessa from failing out of it.

**Tension.** The task is priced honestly and Phillip has honest coin for it. Nessa does not — and
the only way to cover her is to spend something that will actually hurt, in front of everyone,
in the four seconds before iron lands on a Tally child.

**Beats.**

1. **Dawn, the Tally Floor. Phillip, Nessa, three hundred nibs like sleet.** The Michaelmas
   Assize: hold a hundredweight of pig-iron off the boards for a count of sixty. Phillip goes
   first and pays cleanly — he narrates the cold of the winch handle at Lock Four, in more detail
   than the reckoning needs, because saying it is the last time he will have it. It works. He is
   good at this. Establish that the *saying* is the scene.
2. **Nessa's turn.** She offers a thin coin (a wet Tuesday, a bell she half-remembers) because
   she will not spend anything she loves. Vagueness is worthless. The iron slips — toward a Tally
   girl crossing the floor with chalk. Real, physical peril, four seconds long.
3. **Phillip pays for both.** He spends the green of the door of Lock Four with his father
   standing in it — a coin worth ten times the task — and holds the iron. Applause. The
   invigilating Auditor writes *distinguished.* Nessa does not thank him and cannot say why.
   Emotional turn: he is glowing, and underneath the glow he has just made his father a shape
   without a face in a doorway he can no longer see.
4. **The gallery. Bursar Ilsabet Quorn.** Cool, courteous, exact. *"Bond thirty-three. Four times
   what the task required. I have noted it."* She notes it in a small book. It plays as praise. It
   is a purchase order.
5. **Night, the dormitory.** Coldest night of the year; the Counting Bell rings past its count.
   The Sill breath comes up through the boards — bread oven gone out. From far below, a sound like
   a great slow book being turned. It happens twice, and the second time it comes *after* Phillip
   speaks aloud in the dark, as if answering.

**End state.** Fourth-floor dormitory, well after midnight, Phillip barefoot on the boards with
everyone else asleep. Changed: he is one great coin poorer and has been formally *marked* as
willing. He knows spending works and adults reward it; he does not know where the coin went, that
the Bursar is auditioning him, or that the sound below answered his voice specifically. Open
threads: Nessa's unpayable debt to him; the Bursar's little book; the noise under the mountain.

**Hook.** The turning-book sound comes again, nearer — and the floorboards under his bare feet
are *warm*, on the coldest night of the year.

**Payoff seed.** **The green door of Lock Four.** It is the first thing he ever overpaid for, and
in Session 7 it is the last thing the dragon hands back — he stands in a doorway he cannot see
and it comes back green while his father is remembering him. (Second seed: Ilsabet's notebook.
The binding offer in Session 7 begins on this page.)

## SESSION 2 — *Down the Sluice*

**Slug:** `down-the-sluice`

**Logline.** Following the warm draught under the dormitory floor, Phillip and Nessa find the
brick throat that swallows every coin the school spends — and Phillip brings a piece of it back
up in his pocket.

**Goal.** Find out where spent coin goes. (Because if it *goes* somewhere, it can be *fetched*,
and his father's four years are somewhere.)

**Tension.** Access costs. Every door between him and the answer has a price on it, and Phillip
is constitutionally incapable of haggling.

**Beats.**

1. **Dawn, the dormitory.** Phillip pries up the warm board and finds dry grit that glitters. He
   asks the honest question in class and gets the school's answer: *"It is discharged."* Nobody
   asks where.
2. **Odalric "Odd" Pell, introduced.** Sixteen, first in his year, sentences with holes in them —
   *"the thing with the hinges," "the one who does the soup."* He knows about the Sluice and
   considers it plumbing. He will trade the grate key for a favour: Phillip performs a warming
   reckoning Odd is oddly unable to perform himself. Phillip pays without being asked twice. Odd
   regards this as correct behaviour and says so, kindly, which is worse.
3. **The descent, night.** The Sluice: a brick throat, warm and dry, glittering the whole way
   down — scruples in the silt like coin. Phillip touches one bare-handed and lives thirty seconds
   of a stranger's kitchen: flour-dusted hands, a pan burnt black, a child's name called through a
   doorway that is not his. He comes out of it shaking. Nessa will not touch one. *"I'd rather not
   know. That's allowed, Vane."*
4. **Nearly caught; the first arrears.** A porter's lamp on the gallery above. Phillip spends a
   birthday to hold a door closed — and then stands in the dark for three seconds unable to
   remember what he came down for. It passes. He tells Nessa it was nothing. **First lie.**
5. **Up and out.** Boots full of warm grit, ink-black to the elbow. He does not put the scruple
   back.

**End state.** The fourth-floor lamp gallery, about three in the morning; both filthy, Nessa
already walking away toward the stair. Phillip has a stranger's thirty seconds in his pocket and
a lie in his mouth. He knows spent coin does not vanish — it silts, it collects, it can be held
in a hand. He does not know what it becomes, that he is in arrears, or that the kitchen he
touched is a real woman's in a town he can walk to. Open threads: the stolen scruple; Odd's debt
of gratitude; Nessa's refusal, logged.

**Hook.** In the lamplight he looks at the scruple properly, and the smell coming off it is the
Sill breath — bread oven gone out — and it is on his own hands.

**Payoff seed.** **The stolen scruple: Ada Tull's kitchen.** He carries it, unspent, for six
sessions. In Session 7 it is the **first name he reads aloud**, and Ada Tull is alive, and she is
standing in the basin at Cold Gantry when her kitchen comes back to her.

## SESSION 3 — *The Old Girls*

**Slug:** `the-old-girls`

**Logline.** Caught with stolen hoard-stone, Phillip is not reported — he is taken down to meet
where it came from, and at the bottom of the Sumps something with eleven eyes turns all of them
on him.

**Goal.** Get to the bottom. Find out what is down there and whether his father's years are in it.

**Tension.** Hew Marrable is the only adult who tells the truth, and he is also the man who will
choose a dragon over a child. Phillip is safest and least safe in exactly the same company.

**Beats.**

1. **The fourth-floor gallery, immediately.** Auditor Hew Marrable is standing in the lamplight
   with his hand out. He does not report them. *"You've had thirty seconds of Ada Tull's kitchen.
   She lives on Basin Row and she can't remember her own bread. Come and see where it lives now."*
   Nessa hesitates a beat too long — and comes.
2. **The Sumps.** Nine galleries under Barrow Sill. Accruals One through Eight: hides of
   compacted slate and pressed paper, warm as a loaf, hairline seams packed with new matter,
   arcs of slow-blinking eyes each holding a different reflection. Hew talks husbandry —
   *she's off her feed; mind her seams; ease up, ease up.*
3. **The Blank, demonstrated.** Hew walks them to a condemned partition and lets Six breathe on
   it. No fire, no mark, no sound. The wall stops being *that* wall, forgets how to hold a roof,
   and slumps. The draught catches the edge of Nessa's sleeve; Phillip hauls her back by the
   collar. She is quiet for a long time afterward, and something in her decides, permanently,
   that she wants to be on the safe side of that door.
4. **Gallery Nine.** Ninefold. Eleven eyes, and every one of them comes round to Phillip — which
   Hew says, unsettled, he has not seen in thirty years. It does not speak. It hands him back a
   *smell*: bread, wet wool, tallow, and a green door — a kitchen he has never smelled in his
   life — and, under it, a voice saying his name, small. He is crying and does not know why.
5. **The stairhead.** Hew, locking up, says the thing he has printed for thirty years and no one
   with authority has ever acted on: *"They're not beasts, lad. They're people, filed. Somebody
   with a title needs to say that out loud one day, and it won't be me."*

**End state.** The head of the Sump stair, iron door, Hew turning the key; Phillip's hands still
warm from the hide, Nessa two steps up and facing away. He now knows dragons are made of spent
memory and that one of them recognised him. He does not know why, or that the kitchen was his
own at three. Open threads: Ada Tull's scruple still in his pocket; Nessa frightened onto the
Hall's side; Hew now watching Phillip more closely than is comfortable; Phillip knows two ways
down and is locked out of one.

**Hook.** Hew, pocketing the key: *"Don't come back down. And if you do — bring her something.
She doesn't take. She only accepts."*

**Payoff seed.** **Ninefold gave a particular back — freely, unasked, unpaid.** Nothing in
Ilmarch does that. It is the first proof that return is possible without rupture, and it is the
entire mechanism of the choice in Session 7. (Hew's line — *people, filed* — is the sentence
Phillip will finally say out loud, in daylight, in front of the Ledger Houses.)

## SESSION 4 — *A Gift Freely Given*  ⟵ **MIDPOINT REVERSAL**

**Slug:** `a-gift-freely-given`

**Logline.** Phillip goes back down with something unspent to offer, and the dragon answers in a
chorus of borrowed voices — and tells him the one thing that unmakes his entire plan: nothing
bought ever comes back.

**Goal.** Speak with Ninefold, and buy his father's four years out of it.

**Tension.** The offering is not a spell. It ends in no loss — he says it whole and *keeps* it —
which is the opposite of everything the Hall has taught him, and he barely knows how to do it.

**Beats.**

1. **The stairhead, a week on.** Iron door locked; Hew's key gone. Phillip takes the other way —
   the Sluice — alone. He asks Nessa first. She says no, in daylight, out loud, and hates herself
   in a small way that she files where she files everything.
2. **The gift.** Gallery Nine. He offers the one memory nobody else on earth holds: the morning
   after the signing, aged eleven, finding his father sitting on the lock beam with a folded paper,
   crying, and going back inside and never saying he saw. He tells it whole and precise — and it
   does not go. He still has it. He is undone by that.
3. **The chorus.** Ninefold accepts and speaks: clauses in different borrowed cadences, a bargeman's
   vowels giving way mid-word to an old woman's, to a clerk's. *Down. And down. And it does not come
   up. We are what the school throws away.* One clause arrives in a small boy's voice and the
   gallery goes quiet around it.
4. **THE INVERSION.** He asks for his father's four years back and offers to pay any price. The
   answer inverts him: they are *here*, they were not stolen by a monster, they were **filed by the
   school** — and no coin can fetch them, because nothing bought comes back. There is exactly one
   way anything ever leaves a dragon: *I can be read.* His plan — buy it back — is dead at the
   midpoint. The dragon is not the thief. The school is the mouth.
5. **The arrears bite.** Coming up at four in the morning he walks into his own dormitory and does
   not know which bed is his. Odd finds him standing in the dark, counts along the row, puts him in
   the right one, and lies to the inspecting Auditor at bell — because Odd needs Phillip's
   willingness for his own examination, and because Odd knows the symptom intimately and will not
   name it in himself. Neither boy says the word *arrears.*

**End state.** The dormitory at the dawn bell, Odd asleep two beds over, an unsendable letter home
folded under Phillip's mattress (the Bursar's office reads letters "for the school's protection").
He knows dragons are made of what the Hall takes, that his father's four years are inside
Ninefold, that nothing bought returns, and that a dragon can be read. He does not know **who** fed
it whole lives in order, that there are forty-one of them, or that he is one. Open threads: the
gift is now inside the dragon; Odd is covering for him; he cannot remember whether the door at
Lock Four is green or blue — he knows the fact, not the particular.

**Hook.** The last thing Ninefold said, which he will not understand for three more sessions,
spoken in that small boy's voice: *"Read me and I will say your name the way it was said."*

**Payoff seed.** **The gift itself — his father on the lock beam.** It is the only thing Phillip
ever gave instead of spent, and it is inside Ninefold. In Session 7 it comes back to him mid-
reading, seconds before he reads *Tobin Vane*, and it is the reason he can get the name out.

## SESSION 5 — *The Spendhouse Ledger*

**Slug:** `the-spendhouse-ledger`

**Logline.** Phillip breaks into the building where Ilmarch's powerful pay to forget, finds bond
thirty-three in the ledger, and reads what his father actually signed — and the Bursar, who was
always going to be standing behind him, does not deny a single word.

**Goal.** Find out who fed a dragon whole lives in order.

**Tension.** He is chasing a villain and finds an accountant with better arguments than he has.
Ilsabet Quorn is not lying, and Phillip is fifteen, and the arithmetic is good.

**Beats.**

1. **The dormitory, that morning.** Nessa confronts him about where he went. She also tells him
   her news: she has been offered an Auditor's assistantship, early, unusual — a steadying hand on
   a girl who has seen too much. She will still get him out to the Cold Gantry road, because she
   knows the lock path. She will not go through the Spendhouse gate. She sets the limit out loud,
   which is the most honest thing she does in the arc.
2. **Inside the Spendhouse, night.** A banking hall in miniature: brass grille, iron-gall ink,
   waiting-room chairs, a clock. A well-dressed woman comes out unmarked, calm, entirely herself —
   and stands on the step while her driver tells her, gently and clearly used to it, which carriage
   is hers. The dread is in the courtesy.
3. **The ledger.** Forty-one bond entries, each with a sequence number. **Bond 33 — VANE, TOBIN,
   lockkeeper, Gantry Lock Four.** *Sold: the subject's first four years, entire and in sequence.
   Credit applied: tuition, Charterhouse of Reckoning, bond 33.* Not a debt cleared. A price paid.
   Phillip's whole life reorganises itself around one line of copperplate, and his father's
   signature is at the end of it.
4. **Ilsabet Quorn.** She has been in the doorway for some time. She denies nothing. She prices it:
   the Grieving two ridges over — four thousand people handed everything back in one night, ninety-
   one dead inside the week, and she was twenty-three and she was there. *"That would cost the
   valley four hundred lives, Mr Vane. I have done the work. Have you?"* Then the offer: early
   Reckoner, then Auditor, then — one day, when it is needed — **the binding.** She calls it the
   honour. It sounds like being loved.
5. **The road home, dawn.** To keep Nessa's name off the night's report he spends again, in front
   of Ilsabet, who watches and says nothing — because every coin he spends makes him more suitable
   for what she wants him for. He walks up the Cold Gantry road past Lock Four with the lamp lit in
   the window, and he does not go in, because he does not know what he would say.

**End state.** The Cold Gantry road at first light, hard frost, Lock Four's window yellow behind
him, Nessa at the top of the road with a lantern she has been holding a long time. He knows his
place at Dragonhall was bought with his own childhood, that there are forty-one of them, and that
Ilsabet authorised all of it and can defend it. He does not know **why the sequencing mattered**,
or that Ninefold's *I* is his own voice. Open threads: the binding offer, live and unanswered;
Nessa's assistantship; the torn ledger page in his coat.

**Hook.** Up the valley, unrung by any hand, the Counting Bell begins to strike — and does not
stop at the hour.

**Payoff seed.** **The ledger page.** Forty-one names, in sequence, with dates. He takes it. In
Session 7 that page is his **reading order** — the thing that turns a rupture into a recital.

## SESSION 6 — *In Arrears*  ⟵ **LOWEST POINT**

**Slug:** `in-arrears`

**Logline.** Ninefold's seams begin to open, and every person Phillip has ever leaned on pays for
it — Odd catastrophically, Nessa by looking away, Hew by choosing the dragon — and Phillip is sent
home to a father he cannot explain any of it to.

**Goal.** Hold the seam. Keep the valley from a Grieving. Do it by paying, because that is the only
way he has ever known how to help.

**Tension.** For the first time his willingness to pay is not a private wound — it is a licence
other people take up. Odd copies him. Odd cannot afford it. Nobody stops either of them.

**Beats.**

1. **Running up the valley road as the bell strikes.** Phillip and Nessa reach a Hall already
   wrong: the Blank leaking up the Sluice, fourth-floor students sitting on the stairs looking at
   their own hands and not claiming them. Evacuation to the Tally Floor. Hew takes Phillip down
   because Phillip is the only Quire who has been below and did not run.
2. **The seam.** A bracing reckoning at Gallery Nine, load-bearing, ruinous. Phillip pays and pays.
   Odd arrives uninvited, cites Marrable third edition chapter nine, and starts spending — and does
   not stop, because he has never in his life conceived of a cost he ought to decline. He spends
   past his own name. He comes up calling himself *"the — the one with my name on it."* Phillip
   let him, because he needed the help, and because he has believed since Session 1 that paying is
   what love looks like. This is his flaw with somebody else's face on it.
3. **Nessa looks away.** Auditors come to take Odd and to seal the gallery. One witness could say
   what actually happened and who authorised what. Nessa is standing right there in her new
   assistant's armband, and she keeps her post, and says nothing, and knows exactly what she is
   doing while she does it. She calls him *Vane* and cannot manage anything else.
4. **Hew chooses the dragon.** Given the seam or the boy, he packs the seam first. He gets Odd out
   afterward, and afterward is too late for part of Odd. He does not pretend otherwise. Then, low
   and shaking, he gives Phillip the sentence the arc has been building toward: *she was fed in
   order, on purpose, by somebody who wanted her readable — and no one has ever let me try.*
5. **Home, under discipline.** Arrears is a disciplinary matter at Dragonhall, not a medical one;
   Phillip is sent down to Cold Gantry pending review. Tobin meets him at Lock Four with a coat
   too big for him and a task — *"Get that rope in, lad"* — because he turns feeling into work.
   Phillip looks at the door of the lock-house and cannot see that it is green. He knows the fact.
   He sold the particular in Session 1 to be magnificent.

**End state.** The lock-house kitchen at Gantry Lock Four, rain on slate, Tobin asleep in the
chair with his bad knee up, Phillip awake in a borrowed coat. Everything is lost: Odd partly
gone, Nessa's friendship ended by a silence, Hew's true allegiance seen plainly, his own place
suspended, and the dragon days from opening. He knows Ninefold will rupture within days, that the
school's plan is a binding paid by one person spending themselves down to nothing, and that
Ilsabet has already written his name against it. He still does not know that the *I* is his own
voice. Open threads: the ledger page in his coat; the scruple in his pocket; Hew's admission.

**Hook.** The Sill breath reaches Cold Gantry for the first time in living memory — the whole
valley smelling faintly of a bread oven gone out, every dog in four hundred houses awake at once,
and out on the black basin the water going warm.

**Payoff seed.** **Hew's admission that a dragon fed in sequence can be *read*, one life at a
time — and that nobody has ever been permitted to try.** Session 7's second option does not exist
without this sentence. (Second seed: during the rupture a voice comes up through every Sluice
grate in the Hall, and it is a small boy's, and Phillip is the only person in the building who
does not recognise it.)

## SESSION 7 — *The Reading*

**Slug:** `the-reading`

**Logline.** Offered the honour of spending himself to nothing to keep everything standing,
Phillip puts down the chalk, draws no sigil, and begins reading a dragon aloud — name by name,
for as long as it takes — starting with a woman's kitchen and ending with his father's.

**Goal.** Stop the Grieving without becoming the price.

**Tension.** The cheap price is heroic, honourable, and hurts only him. The right one costs
everyone else — Nessa's post, Odd's sponsorship, the school, the valley's paid-for peace — and
hands his father back the afternoon he signed his son away.

**Beats.**

1. **First light, the lock-house.** He leaves before Tobin wakes and walks up the valley under the
   Sill breath. Ilsabet meets him at the Hall gate with the sigil already drawn and the paperwork
   already signed: the binding. Nessa keeps her post if he takes it. Odd keeps his care. Tobin
   never learns what he did, which is a genuine mercy and she says so. Everyone in the courtyard
   is looking at him the way they looked at him on the Tally Floor in Session 1. It is the same
   trap wearing its best coat.
2. **Gallery Nine. The revelation.** Ninefold, at rupture pressure, seams alight with silt. It
   stops quoting. It says ***I*** — and the voice it uses is Phillip Vane at three years old,
   because whole sequenced childhoods are the only thing it was ever given entire, and his is one
   of forty-one, and his is the one it learned to speak with. He is not the dragon's victim. He is
   its **ingredient.**
3. **The choice.** He puts the chalk down. No sigil, no coin, no spend. He takes the torn ledger
   page out of his coat and the stolen scruple out of his pocket, and he reads the first name:
   **Ada Tull, of Basin Row, Cold Gantry** — the flour, the burnt pan, the child called through
   the doorway — out loud, in order, in his own unbought voice. The pressure drops by exactly one
   life.
4. **The cost arrives in real time.** It works, and it is a catastrophe: the Quietage's clients get
   their crimes back with their griefs, in front of witnesses; the Ledger Houses' paper turns to
   nothing; the school's finances end and the school with them. Ilsabet does not stop him — she
   stands and does the sum and finds, for the first time in forty years, that she cannot feel the
   answer. Nessa comes down and this time holds the lamp, which costs her the post and is the only
   apology available to her. Odd, partly, gets one noun back — his own name, handed up out of the
   dark in a stranger's cadence. Hew Marrable sits on the gallery floor and weeps, because someone
   with a voice is finally reading her.
5. **Months. Then the last image.** The reading goes on, publicly, one life at a time. Near the end
   the dragon hands Phillip back the gift he freely gave — his father on the lock beam with the
   folded paper — and he understands, holding it, that he can carry that. Then the last name on the
   page: **Tobin Vane.** Four years go back, entire, in sequence, and they include the afternoon he
   signed. The doorway behind them comes back *green*. His father looks at him and finds something
   there, all of it, the kitchen and the boots and the yard and the paper — and says, for the only
   time in seven sessions: *"Phillip."* They will both have to live with it. That is the point.

**End state.** The Sump floor at Gallery Nine, months on, a lamp, a page, and people from Cold
Gantry sitting on the steps waiting for their names. Dragonhall's Quietage is finished and the
Hall probably with it. Phillip owns nothing, holds a place nowhere, and has spent no coin at all —
his largest act cost him only his future, his safety, and his friends'. Deliberately open: eight
more accruals under Barrow Sill, unread, and one boy's voice to read them with.

**Hook (arc-out).** He turns the page. There are eight more galleries, and the next name on the
list is not from Cold Gantry at all.

**Payoff seed (detonation ledger).** This session fires: **S1** the green door + Ilsabet's
notebook-turned-binding-offer; **S2** Ada Tull's scruple as the first name read; **S3** Ninefold's
free return proving the mechanism and Hew's *people, filed* said out loud at last; **S4** the
freely-given gift handed back, and *"I will say your name the way it was said"* paid in full;
**S5** the ledger page as reading order; **S6** Hew's *fed in order, and nobody let me try.*

## CONTINUITY GUARDS FOR SESSION WRITERS

- **No fire. Anywhere. Ever.** Dragon breath is the Blank.
- Every spell is a **spoken monologue that ends in loss.** Never summarise a spend; stage it.
- The Session 4 **gift** is the one spoken monologue in the arc that ends in *keeping.* Write it
  as the deliberate mirror of every reckoning before it.
- Phillip's overspending is **debt and compulsion**, cheered on by adults who profit — never
  self-injury — and the story names it as harm (Sessions 4 and 6 explicitly).
- The listener should suspect §8's revelation by Session 5 and be certain by Session 6. Session 7's
  pleasure is the **choice**, not the surprise. Do not withhold; withhold only the voice.
- Tobin calls him **lad** in every session and **Phillip** exactly once, in the last beat of
  Session 7.
- Nessa calls him **Vane** throughout, including her last words in Session 6. She says
  **Phillip** for the first time in Session 7, at the lamp.
- Each session ends on a held breath or a quiet ache. Land it soft, even when it lands hard.
